I'm a Logistics & Supply Chain Management graduate-in-progress at RMIT Vietnam, pairing a Finance minor with three years of large-scale operational leadership. I've scaled a 150+ person student organisation to a 92% retention rate, run event logistics for recruitment days of 4,000+ participants, and led a supply chain digital-transformation project with CVI Pharma — the common thread is turning messy, people-heavy operations into systems that actually run.

My double major gives me fluency in both sides of a supply chain problem: the physical flow of goods, distribution networks, and fleet routing on one hand, and capital allocation, cost modelling, and financial risk on the other. I'm most energised by problems that sit at that intersection — reducing empty miles, forecasting demand, and building the data infrastructure that lets a logistics operation scale without breaking.

My double major in Logistics & Supply Chain Management and Finance wasn't a hedge between two unrelated fields — it's the same lens applied twice: how do resources move, and how is that movement financed and valued? That's shown up directly in this portfolio. The courses I've performed strongest in are the ones that map onto the projects here: Transportation & Freight Logistics onto the Ferntree carrier comparison, Supply Chain Technologies onto the CVI Pharma systems study, Forecasting and Quantitative Analysis onto the SCFI freight-rate model, and Operations Management onto the PikPik production plan. My grades improved as the coursework moved from foundational theory toward applied, decision-focused subjects — which tracks with how I actually work: I learn a framework faster when I can see what decision it's supposed to support.

Leadership at RMIT hasn't been one role — it's been a track that ran alongside the coursework, across Career Ready Assistant, RQAC, the Student Ambassador Team, and CEC, each one handing me a different group of people to plan for and be responsible to: a handful of teammates, then 100+ SAT members, then an entire club's calendar of events. The SAT Lead From Within Award (2025) is the milestone I'd point to for what that adds up to — it specifically recognizes impact made from inside the organization: engaging members in the community, connecting people with each other, and supporting members at risk of disengaging. The RMIT Leadership Excellence Award (2026) is the university-wide version of that same recognition, and it's what selected me for the RMIT Global Impact Student Leadership Program in Singapore — the milestone I'd point to for where three years of building things for other people has led.
